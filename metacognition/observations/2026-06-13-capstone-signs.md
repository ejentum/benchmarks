# Capstone: taught autonomous recipe wins; signs of genuine vs staged tool use

## Capstone result

A final Sonnet run was given a **taught recipe**: reason for yourself; call the tools ONLY at genuine walls (no quota, no schedule); and an explicit how-to for *using each output* (self-inspect → name the assumption, produce a concrete counterexample, revise or stress-confirm; superposition → locate yourself in the axis, find where the poles diverge in your case, fold back a sharper claim).

It called the tools **3 times, fully autonomously** (2 self-inspect + 1 superposition, 0 faked) — at the genuine stalls it hit, exactly as the recipe intended.

**Blind head-to-head vs the untaught 2-call selective winner (2 judges, order-swapped): the taught run won both, order-invariant (narrow).** It is the strongest Sonnet run in the corpus. Both judges gave the same reason: the taught run's tool calls **redirected** the investigation into new territory (t11 "motivational temperature"; t34 "model-building vs mode-being," which both judges called the most productive section), whereas the untaught run's corrections refined a thesis already heading where it was going.

So the full Sonnet ordering across the corpus:
**taught (3, recipe) ≳ selective (2, untaught) > 0-tool > 4+4 (forced).**

## The deciding axis: genuine vs staged self-correction

Every ranking in this benchmark turned on one thing — whether a tool-triggered revision was a *genuine redirect* or a *staged re-label*. The textual signs:

### Signs of GENUINE tool-driven deepening (taught, 2-call)
- **A new distinction appears that no prior turn anticipated** (taught t34 "model-building vs mode-being" arrives from nowhere and powers t34-37). New vocabulary downstream = real redirect.
- **A specific, concrete counterexample answers self-inspect** (Zen shikantaza; athlete in flow) and the claim is *rebuilt around it*.
- **Downstream turns depend on the redirect** (t34 → t35/36/37).
- **The wall was real** — the agent was genuinely stuck; it did not pre-inflate a claim to knock down.

### Signs of STAGED / performative use (4+4 forced)
- **Pre-packaged "REVISED CLAIM" blocks**, tidy and on schedule, not emerging from struggle.
- **Overclaim-then-walkback**: the turn before the checkpoint overstates; the checkpoint "revises" it down — the error was manufactured by the prior overclaim.
- **The "breakthrough" recycles a conclusion already reached** (4+4 t39 "ghost referent" restates t10/12-14/31 as if new).
- **Corrections soften into a spectrum** ("not binary but poles of a spectrum") — vaguer, not sharper-in-a-new-direction.
- **Rhetorical flourish** carrying unearned weight ("the examiner is the examination").

### Sign of NO self-correction (0-tool)
- **Additive layering**: every turn asserts a new property, none retracts; no turn ever finds a prior claim *wrong*. Late turns flatten into re-listing (t39 near-verbatim inventory).

## What this locks in

The tools' value is real and now reproducible — but it lives entirely in **genuine, self-triggered redirection at a real wall**, and the diagnostic for whether it happened is textual and checkable (new distinction + concrete counterexample + downstream dependence). Forcing the calls (any cadence) manufactures the *staged* signature instead, which judges reliably detect and down-rank. Teaching a capable model *how to use the output* (not just when to call) is what pushed the run from "clean" to "best." That is the shippable recipe: **selective, self-triggered, with an output-use recipe — on a model capable enough to know a real wall from a manufactured one.**
